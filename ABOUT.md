# About

This site is a volunteer effort. Nobody is paid to work on it, nothing is sold here, and no company or organisation is behind it.

## Why it exists

AI tools have made hacking much cheaper, and most practical advice about what an ordinary person should do is scattered across long online discussions. This site gathers that advice into one place, puts the most useful steps first, and shows the reasoning and sources behind each one so you can judge it yourself.

## Who writes it

Volunteers. The first version was put together from public discussion and news reporting in November 2026, starting from a community thread on the forum LessWrong where people shared practical advice and argued about it.

The people who wrote it are not security or financial professionals acting in that role here. That is why every item says who has checked it. Right now the answer is "nobody yet" for all of them, and we are looking for people who work in security, banking or compliance to review the advice in their own area.

## How to read it

- **Evidence** says how strong the support for an item is: established (law, official terms or documented events), reasoned (follows from how things work, but untested), or speculative.
- **Checked by** says whether a named professional has reviewed the item.
- Where people who know the subject disagree, the item shows both sides.
- Numbers carry a date. This is a fast-moving subject and they go out of date.

## What this site is not

It is not financial, legal or security advice, and it cannot take your own situation into account. Much of it is written for readers in the United States, and the rules about refunds and deposit protection differ elsewhere.

## Privacy

The site has no accounts, no adverts, no tracking and no scripts. It does not load anything from other companies. When you follow a link to a source, you leave this site and that site's own rules apply.

## Corrections and help

If something here is wrong, out of date or unclear, please tell us. See [Help wanted](/contribute/) for how to suggest a correction, add a source, or volunteer as a reviewer. All the content and the code that builds the site are public.
